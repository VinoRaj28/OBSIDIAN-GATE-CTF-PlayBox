Case Note 1 — Digital Footprint+
Everyone leaves something behind when they walk out a door. Cole's not on the payroll anymore, but he's still on the internet. Start with what the company itself says about him — then look past what it says. People rarely scrub everything. Sometimes what they forget to wipe is sitting in the file itself, not on the page.
Case Note 2 — Hidden in Plain Sight+
He mentioned a backup. Casual, almost bragging — the kind of thing people say when they think no one's paying attention. Two files, apparently. One's worth your time, one isn't. And that badge number he can't stop reusing? Old habits don't just describe a person. Sometimes they unlock things.
Case Note 3 — The Vendor Portal+
He all but told us where the company's weak point was, in his own words. An internal tool nobody hardened properly. Doors that ask politely for a password sometimes open if you ask the question differently — if you know how questions can be broken.
Case Note 4 — The Comms Archive+
Somewhere in the mess of internal tickets, one number keeps surfacing — the same one attached to his badge. Follow it far enough and there's a message, wrapped twice over so a casual glance won't make sense of it. Whoever wrote it wasn't hiding from a machine. They were hiding from someone who wasn't paying close attention.
Case Note 5 — The Returned Device+
Company policy: every device gets imaged before it's wiped, no exceptions. Cole's laptop went through the process like everyone else's — except something was missing when it came back. Deleted doesn't mean destroyed. It just means harder to find, not impossible.
Case Note 6 — Full Compromise+
Somewhere in that decoded message was more than words — a way in. His own machine, not the company's. People who run things themselves get comfortable. Comfortable people leave scripts lying around that anyone can edit, and forget that someone else is always watching the clock too.
Dependency: Case Note 6 requires the credentials found in Case Note 4.
